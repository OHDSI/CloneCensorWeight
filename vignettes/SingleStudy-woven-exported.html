library(CloneCensorWeight)

Study design

Clinical question

Among adults with rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, or ulcerative colitis who newly initiate a JAK inhibitor, does receiving the recombinant zoster vaccine (RZV, Shingrix) within 3 months of JAK inhibitor initiation reduce incident herpes zoster over 2 years, compared with no vaccination during that window?

Why this question requires CCW

JAK inhibitors substantially increase herpes zoster risk. Clinical guidelines recommend RZV prior to or at the time of JAK inhibitor initiation, but real-world vaccination rates are low and timing is variable. A naive comparison of vaccinated vs. unvaccinated JAK inhibitor users is vulnerable to two problems.

Immortal time bias. A patient who receives RZV on day 60 has survived 60 days without herpes zoster before entering the “vaccinated” group. Assigning all their follow-up to the vaccinated group credits the vaccine with 60 days of event-free time it did not cause.

Confounding by indication. Patients who receive RZV tend to be older, more engaged with preventive care, and treated by more proactive providers — all of which independently predict herpes zoster risk. Standard baseline covariate adjustment cannot correct for confounding that evolves over the follow-up window.

CCW addresses both problems. Every patient is cloned into both arms simultaneously at cohort entry, so baseline balance is guaranteed. A clone is censored only when its observed behavior deviates from its assigned protocol. Inverse probability of censoring weighting corrects for thee informative nature of that censoring.

Target trial

Element Specification
Eligibility Adults with RA, PsA, or UC; new JAK inhibitor user (none in prior 365 days); no prior RZV in past 5 years; ≥ 365 days prior observation
Arm 1 Receive first dose of RZV within 90 days of JAK inhibitor start
Arm 2 No RZV within the first 90 days (vaccination after day 90 is permitted)
Assignment Cloning at JAK inhibitor start date
Follow-up Until herpes zoster, death, disenrollment, or 730 days
Outcome Incident herpes zoster
Causal contrast Per-protocol effect of early vaccination vs. deferred or no vaccination

Reading across the table clarifies several key properties of CCW:

  • Guaranteed balance at baseline. Every subject enters both arms on the same date with the same prior history; there is no baseline confounding by construction.

  • Complementary censoring. Subjects 2, 3, and 5 follow the no_rzv_within90 protocol and are censored from arm rzv_within90 at day 90. Subjects 1 and 4 follow the rzv_within90 protocol and are censored from arm no_rzv_within90 at their initiation day. After day 90 every subject’s observed behavior is consistent with exactly one arm.

  • Outcome credit goes to the arm that earned it. Subject 4 received RZV on day 60 and developed herpes zoster on day 213. That outcome appears only in the rzv_within90 arm — the no_rzv_within90 clone was already censored at day 60. The mirror image holds for Subject 5: because they never vaccinated, their outcome on day 152 accrues entirely to the no_rzv_within90 arm.

  • IPCW corrects for informative censoring. The artificial censoring above is not random: subjects who are healthy and well-managed are more likely to receive RZV promptly, so the subjects who survive in the rzv_within90 arm are a selected group. The inverse probability of censoring weights estimated in the next phase reweight each arm back to the uncensored target population.

Creating the analysis settings

Every analytical choice in a CCW study is captured in a hierarchy of R6 settings objects. This design allows specifications to be validated at construction time, serialized to JSON, and shared or reproduced across sites and Strategus pipelines before any data are touched.

Treatment strategy arms

createArmDefinition() specifies the protocol a clone in each arm is assigned to follow. The strategyType determines the censoring rule applied when observed data deviate from the protocol:

  • "initiateWithin" — censored if treatment has not occurred by day gracePeriod.
  • "deferBeyond" — censored if treatment occurs within day gracePeriod; initiation after that window is consistent with the strategy.

Together these form a grace period comparison: once the 90-day window closes, every patient’s observed behavior is consistent with exactly one arm.

armVaccinate <- createArmDefinition(
  armId        = "rzv_within90",
  strategyType = "initiateWithin",
  gracePeriod  = 90,
  label        = "RZV within 90 days of JAK inhibitor start"
)

armDeferred <- createArmDefinition(
  armId        = "no_rzv_within90",
  strategyType = "deferBeyond",
  gracePeriod  = 90,
  label        = "No RZV within 90 days"
)

Full analysis specification

createCcwAnalysis() assembles all step-level objects into a single, validated, serializable specification.

analysis <- createCcwAnalysis(
  analysisId                     = "main_90day",
  description                    = "Primary: RZV within 90 days vs. deferred",
  getDbCloneCensorWeightDataArgs = dbArgs,
  createStudyPopulationArgs      = studyPopArgs,
  createClonesAndCensorArgs      = clonesArgs,
  createCensoringWeightsArgs     = weightsArgs,
  fitOutcomeModelArgs            = outcomeModelArgs
)

Target cohort and outcomes

createTargetArmOutcome() links the analysis to specific cohort definition IDs in the database. Negative control outcomes are included alongside the primary outcome to support empirical calibration.

#  101      New JAK inhibitor users with RA, PsA, or UC
#  102      First dose of RZV — used for both arms; strategyType determines
#           how each arm interprets initiation events
#  201      Incident herpes zoster (primary outcome)
#  301-320  Negative control outcomes for empirical calibration

tao <- createTargetArmOutcome(
  targetId                    = 101L,
  arm1CohortId                = 102L,
  arm2CohortId                = 102L,
  outcomeIds                  = c(201L, 301:320),
  excludedCovariateConceptIds = rzvConceptIds
)

Top-level specifications

createCcwAnalysesSpecifications() combines all analyses and target-arm-outcome definitions into the object accepted by runCcwAnalyses().

specs <- createCcwAnalysesSpecifications(
  ccwAnalysisList      = list(analysis),
  targetArmOutcomeList = list(tao)
)
